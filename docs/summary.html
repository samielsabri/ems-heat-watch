EMS Heat Watch

Can paramedic data give early warning of heat's health impact, and measure the burden that heat-coded records miss? A proof of concept on public New York City data.

What I did

  • Data: daily NYC ambulance calls, 2010 to September 2026: coded heat (about 800 a year), and heat-related calls with other codes chosen in advance (unconscious, altered mental status, sick, breathing, cardiac, respiratory, arrest). Central Park Tmax.
  • Early warning: each day, compare heat calls with the number expected on a normal-temperature day at that time of year; alert when above the expected range. Tested by replaying 2016–2026 day by day on past data only, against every heat wave (3+ days at 32 °C or more). Nothing was tuned on 2025–26.
  • Hidden burden: the same model on the other heat-related calls; during each heat wave, compare their excess over a normal-temperature day with the excess in heat-coded calls.

*Outside heat waves. Farrington Flexible is the standard method for routine communicable disease surveillance; EARS is the CDC's syndromic method (both from the R surveillance package). The new indicator catches more heat waves than Farrington for about two extra alerts a summer.

Hidden burden: across 23 heat waves, the model attributes 2,752 extra heat-coded calls and 5,470 extra calls with other heat-related codes, so only about a third of the heat-associated excess (34%) is labelled as heat. Raw counts suggest even less (17%). The other calls were above normal in 20 of 23 heat waves.

What it could mean for Toronto

  • ECCC warns 18–24 hours ahead for heat events of 2+ days. Paramedic data adds the health side: is heat causing harm, and how much, including harm not labelled as heat. Single hot days rarely get a warning; the indicator flagged 49% of them.
  • Plan: aggregated daily Toronto Paramedic Services counts by call type; humidex; Toronto's 158 neighbourhoods. Open question: does Toronto's dispatch record a heat or exposure code? If not, the other-codes approach carries the signal.

Limits

  • Dispatch codes record what callers and crews report, not diagnoses.
  • The other-codes excess is noisy: below normal in 3 of 23 heat waves; 2021–22 baselines are pulled down by 2020.
  • Three held-out heat waves. NYC data, one weather station; not yet tested in Toronto.
