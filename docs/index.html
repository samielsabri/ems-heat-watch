EMS Heat Watch

Can ambulance calls show the health impact of heat within days? Daily NYC ambulance calls coded heat, compared with what a normal-temperature day would bring. Alarms mark days with more heat calls than expected.

Heat waves

Heat wave: 3 or more days in a row at 32.2 °C (90 °F) or above, Central Park. Cells show the day of the heat wave on which each method first raised an alarm (day 0 = first day), or ✗ if it raised none.

All methods, all years

2016–24 was used to build the indicator; 2025–26 was held out. False alarms are alarms on cool summer days (May–Sep, Tmax under 29.4 °C that day and the three before, away from heat waves).

How it works

Each week a Poisson regression is fitted to the previous three years of daily heat calls (season, day of week, trend, and temperature above a threshold). It gives the number of heat calls expected if the temperature were normal for that date. An alarm is raised when calls exceed the 99.5% upper limit of that expectation. Farrington Flexible and EARS C1–C3 come from the R surveillance package and use the call counts only.

Limits: dispatch codes record what callers and crews report, not diagnoses. One weather station, maximum temperature only. Mar–Jul 2020 (COVID) is excluded. NYC's public data is released in batches, weeks behind.
